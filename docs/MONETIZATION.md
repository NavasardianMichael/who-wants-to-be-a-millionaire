# Monetization — strategy notes

*Discussion draft, 2026-11-03. Nothing here is built yet; the codebase has no billing or ad code.*

What the app is, for this purpose: a free web PWA with no install, 80 languages, cheap
marginal cost per player because questions are generated once and reused, and ranked
leaderboards whose value depends on being fair.

## Check this before charging anything: trademark exposure

The format belongs to Sony Pictures Television: the 15-step ladder to $1,000,000, safe havens,
50:50, "Ask the Audience" and "Phone a Friend". The show title is already kept out of visible
copy. But once the app makes money, the show title in the keywords meta, JSON-LD and
`llms.txt` counts as commercial use, and some lifeline names may be registered marks.

- A short consultation with an IP lawyer before launching paid features is worth it.
- Cheap fixes: generic lifeline names, and dropping the show title from metadata.

## Recommended model

**Free to play, ads first, a cheap subscription on top, with ranked play never for sale.**

Casual trivia games usually convert only about 0.5–2% of players to paying. So ads bring in
most of the money, and the subscription is mainly a way for players to remove them.

### 1. Ads

| Placement | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display (AdSense) | Server-rendered pages: categories, leaderboard, sample questions, profile | Low effort. Watch the effect on Core Web Vitals, which affect SEO. |
| Interstitial | Between games, never between questions | Google's H5 Games Ads (Ad Placement API) supports this on the web. |
| Rewarded video | Classic only: "watch an ad for an extra lifeline" or "a second chance after a wrong answer" | Usually the best-earning and best-liked ad format. A second chance after missing a big question is an emotional moment, so it's a strong offer. |

Ads bring obligations:

- A certified consent banner (CMP) for EU and UK players.
- Rules for children's audiences (COPPA). Trivia does attract under-13s.

### 2. "Plus" subscription

What it could include:

- **No ads**: the main reason most people subscribe.
- **Answer explanations**: a short "why this is right", generated by Gemini in the player's
  language. It costs money per question, which is why it belongs behind a paywall, and it
  fits a learning angle.
- **Detailed stats**: accuracy by category and level, history and trends.
- **Cosmetics**: board and studio themes, avatar frames, a highlighted name on the
  leaderboard, sound packs.
- **Private leagues**: a friends-only leaderboard for Marathon and Time Attack.
- **Language-learning mode**: play in a target language with hints in your own. Very few
  competitors can offer this, because the questions are written natively in 80 languages.

Suggested pricing:

| Plan | Price (US) |
|---|---|
| Monthly | $2.99 |
| Yearly | $19.99 |
| Lifetime | $34.99 |

Lower prices in poorer markets. Many of the locales (am, ha, ig, om, my, km, ne…) are in
markets where $3 is a lot. Roughly 30–50% of the US price for tier-3 countries is typical.

### 3. Hosted events for groups (later, potentially the biggest opportunity)

A "host a show" mode: the game runs on a TV or projector and contestants play on their
phones. Sell it to pub quizzes, corporate team-building, classrooms and parties:

- $9–15 per event, or
- $10–30/month for venues and teachers.

Kahoot shows that schools and businesses will pay for this kind of thing, and the TV-show
format suits a big screen better than any other trivia style. It's the largest build of
these options, so it belongs after there is traffic.

### 4. Smaller options

- **Sponsored categories or seasonal tournaments**: a brand pays for "the [Brand] Football
  Cup". Non-cash prizes only; cash prizes turn into sweepstakes or gambling law in 80
  countries.
- **Licensing the question bank**: selling verified multilingual questions to other
  developers. Check Gemini's terms on how its output can be used.

## What to avoid

- **Selling anything that affects ranked results** (lifelines, time, retries in Marathon or
  Time Attack). A fair leaderboard is what makes those modes worth playing.
- **Energy or lives systems**: they feel hostile in a free web game and drive players away.
- **Loot boxes**: regulated or banned in places like Belgium and the Netherlands.
- **Locking features that are free today**, such as Classic settings or offline play. Put
  new features behind the paywall instead.

## Platform choice affects fees

- **Web only:** a merchant of record like Paddle or Lemon Squeezy. They handle VAT in every
  country and charge about 5% + $0.50 per sale.
- **Google Play (the PWA wrapped as a TWA) or the App Store:** both require their own in-app
  billing, which takes 15–30%.

Starting web-only keeps more of each sale. The stores can be added later for discovery.

## Suggested order

1. **Now:** consent banner, AdSense on content pages, rewarded ads in Classic. Measure
   revenue per daily player.
2. **At a few thousand daily players:** launch Plus (no ads, explanations, stats,
   cosmetics) with regional pricing through Paddle.
3. **Later:** host mode for events and classrooms, plus sponsorships.

## Open questions

- What traffic is there now, and from which countries?
- Side income, or a business worth investing in?
- Are ads acceptable at all, given that they'd make the game look less premium?
- Selling to schools and events (host mode), or keeping it purely consumer?
